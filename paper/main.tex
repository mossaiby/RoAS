\title{Reads over Atom Spaces:\\
A Mathematical View of Context, Retrieval, and Parameter Routing in Language Models}

\author[inst1]{Farshid Mossaiby\corref{cor1}}
\ead{mossaiby@eng.ui.ac.ir}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\affiliation[inst1]{organization={Department of Civil Engineering, University of Isfahan}, postcode={81744-73441}, city={Isfahan}, country={Iran}}

\begin{abstract}
Attention, external retrieval, and parameter-expert routing all compute query-conditioned weighted reads. We provide a common analytical lens based on valued atom spaces $(\Om,\mathcal F,\nu,v)$ and the read operator $R(x;\Om)=\int_\Om v(\omega)\,d\mu_x(\omega)$, which reduces to a softmax-weighted value combination under a counting measure. \rev{Operations on the reference measure $\nu$ express several mechanisms in one place. A \emph{null atom} yields abstention, and the hard activation threshold of gated retrieval is its zero-temperature limit. A matched \emph{anti-atom} is exactly deletion plus a null mass at the deleted key. A query-side margin certifies, at insertion time and without a Lipschitz premise, a radius within which a record remains the top-1 read.} An exact softmax-tail bound gives an error-budgeted truncation rule that selects 52--86\% of a synthetic corpus at $N=25{,}000$, so it is a certificate rather than a sublinear method. On CounterFact-500, held-out calibration of a frozen-MiniLM activation threshold raises paraphrase activation from 38.2\% to 53.2\% at a false-activation cost of 9.6\% to 16.1\%. \rev{The remaining gap to 91\% top-1 recall consists of correctly ranked, low-similarity paraphrases: a gate on the rank margin reaches 70.8\% activation at 7.0\% false activation. Under one scorer, the gated read, a rank-one parametric reference, and GRACE occupy different points of a generalization--locality trade-off set by their key spaces.} Tri-space routing with null atoms and first-token steering are reported as diagnostics. We offer a shared vocabulary and empirical calibrations of what retrieval, margin, and tail arguments can certify, not a new editing method.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autoregressive language models encode linguistic abstractions, syntax, and factual knowledge within dense parameter matrices \cite{vaswani2017attention, brown2020language, geva2021transformer}. While gradient-based optimization successfully acquires structural linguistic generalizations, compiling dynamic factual statements directly into static weights presents persistent operational challenges. Real-world facts obsolesce rapidly, require targeted localized updates, and are subject to regulatory erasure mandates (e.g., the ``Right to be Forgotten''). Consequently, post-hoc weight modification often triggers catastrophic interference, parameter drift, or high computational costs \cite{meng2022locating, meng2023mass}.

To address the rigidity of dense parameter storage, multiple architectural strategies have emerged across distinct sub-disciplines. In-context reasoning caches dynamic token representations directly within the key-value sequence memory of the model \cite{vaswani2017attention}. In parallel, retrieval-augmented generation (RAG) and nearest-neighbor language models ($k$NN-LM) decouple memory from parameters by querying external vector databases of textual passages \cite{lewis2020rag, khandelwal2020generalization, borgeaud2022improving}. Meanwhile, modular computation mechanisms, including Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) architectures and parameter-efficient adapter banks, dynamically route tokens across banks of specialized sub-networks \cite{shazeer2017outrageously, hu2021lora, jiang2024mixtral}. Although these three paradigms all rely fundamentally on key-value similarity matching \cite{geva2021transformer, lample2019large, tsai2019transformer}, they are conventionally formulated using disconnected notations, disparate routing heuristics, and distinct operational semantics.

In this work, we adopt a single analytical lens for in-context attention, external retrieval, and parameter-expert output routing: each is an integral of a value map against a query-conditioned probability measure over a \emph{valued atom space}. Under a counting measure this read is exactly a softmax-weighted combination, so the lens is a change of notation rather than a new algorithm, and we do not claim that the three mechanisms are algorithmically interchangeable. Related expectation-based views of attention appear in kernel attention \cite{tsai2019transformer} and sparse-transform attention \cite{martins2020sparse}; our contribution is to use one formalism across the three memory locations, so that insertion, truncation, cancellation, and calibration questions can be posed once and tested on each.

\paragraph{Contributions}
\rev{An integral form of attention is not new. Martins et al.~\cite{martins2020sparse} define attention over a general measure space as the expectation of a value function under a query-dependent density, which reduces to softmax attention under the counting measure; Tsai et al.~\cite{tsai2019transformer} analyse attention as a kernel smoother, and Katharopoulos et al.~\cite{katharopoulos2020transformers} rewrite it with kernel feature maps; and Vuckovic et al.~\cite{vuckovic2020mathematical} and Geshkovski et al.~\cite{geshkovski2025mathematical} model attention and Transformers as maps on probability measures. Our claim is narrower: a few operations on the reference measure $\nu$ (separate normalization per space, signed atoms, and null atoms) express, and let us analyse on one footing, three mechanisms that are usually described separately, namely in-context attention, external retrieval, and parameter-expert routing.} The paper makes the following contributions.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\roman*)]
\item Section~\ref{sec:atomspaces} defines the read operator $R(x;\Om)$, specializes it to context, corpus, and parameter-expert spaces, and derives from the measure view the requirement that spaces of very different cardinality be normalized separately and combined by a gate, with unit-norm keys across spaces.
\item \rev{Section~\ref{sec:null_atoms} introduces null atoms and shows that the hard activation threshold of gated retrieval is the zero-temperature limit of one null atom (Proposition~\ref{prop:null_atom}); this gives the tri-space router an explicit abstention outcome. Section~\ref{sec:editing} shows that a matched anti-atom equals deletion plus a null mass at the deleted key (Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_vs_delete}), which identifies exactly when the two differ.}
\item \rev{Section~\ref{sec:editing} gives a query-side certified radius (Proposition~\ref{prop:query_radius}): a record that is top-1 for its canonical query remains top-1, and activates, for every query within a radius computable at insertion time from the stored keys. We measure how much of CounterFact this certificate covers.}
\item Section~\ref{sec:quadrature} records the exact softmax-tail bound for top-$k$ truncation and the resulting error-budgeted stopping rule. \rev{The bound is elementary; the empirical content is the certified depth, which is 52--86\% of a synthetic corpus at $N=25{,}000$.}
\item Section~\ref{sec:counterfact} evaluates calibrated gated retrieval on CounterFact-500 with explicit counting rules, \rev{a failure analysis of the rejected-but-correct and falsely activated queries, and a key-construction ablation}. The same 500 records are scored for the read, a rank-one parametric update, and GRACE \cite{hartvigsen2023aging} \rev{with one scoring function}.
\end{enumerate}
The remaining experiments (tri-space routing and GPT-2 first-token steering) are controlled mechanism studies whose limitations we state where they appear.

\section{From a Discrete Function to an Integral}
\label{sec:discrete_to_integral}

\subsection{The Token-Level Function}

A language model is conventionally defined as a conditional probability distribution over a finite vocabulary $\V$ of size $d$, given an input sequence. Fixing a maximum context capacity $c$ and introducing a null padding symbol $\varnothing$ yields an augmented vocabulary $\VBar = \V \cup \{\varnothing\}$. An input sequence is represented as a tuple
\[
  x = (x_1, \dots, x_c) \in \VBar^{\,c},
\]
where unassigned sequence positions are padded with $\varnothing$. A language model is a mapping
\[
  p : \VBar^{\,c} \to \Delta^{d-1}, \qquad
  \Delta^{d-1} = \Big\{ z \in \R^d : z_i \ge 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^d z_i = 1 \Big\}.
\]

\subsection{Attention as an Integral}

Let $\phi : \VBar \to \R^{e}$ denote an embedding map with $\phi(\varnothing) = 0$. Extending the sequence to a step function over the continuous interval $[0,c]$ gives:
\[
  f_x(t) = \phi\big(x_{\lceil t \rceil}\big), \qquad t \in (0,c],
\]
with $f_x(0) = \phi(x_1)$. Let $\alpha_x : [0,c] \to \R_{\ge 0}$ be an attention weight density satisfying $\int_0^c \alpha_x(t)\,dt = 1$, with $\alpha_x(t) = 0$ whenever $x_{\lceil t \rceil} = \varnothing$. The context representation vector is:
\[
  z(x) = \int_0^c \alpha_x(t)\, f_x(t)\, dt \in \R^e,
\]
and the corresponding token prediction distribution is $p(x) = \mathrm{softmax}\big( W z(x) + b \big)$.
Because $f_x$ is piecewise constant over unit intervals, the continuous integral evaluates algebraically to the standard discrete attention weighted sum \cite{vaswani2017attention, martins2020sparse}:
\[
  \int_0^c \alpha_x(t)\, f_x(t)\, dt = \sum_{i=1}^c \left( \int_{i-1}^i \alpha_x(t)\, dt \right) \phi(x_i) = \sum_{i=1}^c \alpha_i \phi(x_i).
\]
This continuous formulation naturally generalizes by replacing the sequence index interval $[0,c]$ with an arbitrary measurable \emph{atom space}.

\section{Atom Spaces: A Common Object for Context, Retrieval, and Parameters}
\label{sec:atomspaces}

\begin{definition}[Valued Atom Space]
A \emph{valued atom space} is a tuple $(\Om, \mathcal{F}, \nu, v)$, where $(\Om,\mathcal{F})$ is a measurable space of elements designated as \emph{atoms}, $\nu$ is a $\sigma$-finite reference measure, and $v : \Om \to \R^e$ is a measurable \emph{value map} into a common output representation space.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Query-Conditioned Kernel and Read Operator]
Given a query $x\in\mathcal X$, let $K_x : \Om \to \R_{\ge 0}$ be measurable with
\[
  0 < Z_x \coloneqq \int_\Om K_x(\omega)\,d\nu(\omega) < \infty.
\]
Then $K_x$ induces a query-conditioned probability measure $\mu_x$ on $(\Om, \mathcal{F})$ via the Radon--Nikodym derivative:
\[
  d\mu_x(\omega) = \frac{K_x(\omega)}{Z_x}\, d\nu(\omega).
\]
If $\int_\Om\|v(\omega)\|_2\,d\mu_x(\omega)<\infty$, the \emph{read} conditioned on $x$ is the Bochner integral:
\[
  R(x; \Om) = \int_\Om v(\omega)\, d\mu_x(\omega) \in \R^e.
\]
\end{definition}

When $\nu$ is a discrete counting measure over a finite set $\Om = \{\omega_1, \dots, \omega_N\}$, the Bochner integral evaluates directly to a softmax-weighted sum:
\[
  R(x; \Om) = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{K_x(\omega_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^N K_x(\omega_j)} v(\omega_i).
\]
As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:instances}, in-context attention, dense retrieval, and expert-output routing are structural realizations of this read operator, differing in their domain of integration and the query-dependence of their values.

\begin{remark}[What the measure view does and does not add]
Every result in this paper is proved on finite sums and could be stated without measure theory. The measure view is retained for three reasons. First, the reference measure $\nu$ is the natural carrier of per-record mass, so insertion, reweighting, and (via signed $\nu$) cancellation become operations on $\nu$ alone with $K_x$ and $v$ fixed (Section~\ref{sec:signed_spaces}). Second, the normalization $Z_x=\int K_x\,d\nu$ makes explicit that spaces with $|\Om|$ differing by many orders of magnitude cannot share one normalizer (Section~\ref{sec:calibration}). Third, the same object $\mu_x$ is what an activation threshold, a top-$k$ truncation, and a routing gate all act on, which lets Sections~\ref{sec:editing}--\ref{sec:quadrature} reuse one set of definitions across all three spaces.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The Unified Architecture}

Context, external corpus memory, and parameter routing combine into an integrated representation:
\[
  z(x) = \lambda_{\mathrm{ctx}}(x) R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{ctx}}) 
       + \lambda_{\mathrm{corpus}}(x) R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}) 
       + \lambda_{\mathrm{params}}(x) R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{params}}),
\]
\[
  p(x) = \mathrm{softmax}\big( W_0\, z(x) + b_0 \big),
\]
where $\lambda(x) \in \Delta^2$ is an input-dependent gating distribution across the three spaces, and $W_0 \in \R^{d \times e}, b_0 \in \R^d$ represent the linguistic projection head. For parameter experts, the value is query-dependent, $v_x(\omega)=g_\omega(x)$; the read is therefore an output-space mixture, not an average of parameter tensors.

\subsection{System, Calibration, and Tying Principles}
\label{sec:calibration}

Translating the tri-space read formulation into a functional architecture requires addressing scale calibration and representation tying. Because the cardinalities of the respective spaces vary across orders of magnitude---ranging from $|\Om_{\mathrm{ctx}}| \sim 10^3$ tokens in context and $|\Om_{\mathrm{params}}| \sim 10^1$ modular experts to $|\Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}| \sim 10^9$ external passages---normalizing a single joint softmax across their disjoint union $\Om_{\mathrm{ctx}} \sqcup \Om_{\mathrm{corpus}} \sqcup \Om_{\mathrm{params}}$ would cause the most populous space to artificially dominate probability mass. To preserve balanced access, separate intra-space measure normalization is coupled with calibrated convex gating $\lambda(x)$. Crucially, key representations across distinct spaces must share identical geometric scales through unit $L_2$-normalization, which prevents differing dot-product magnitudes from biasing the router toward unnormalized representations.

Furthermore, for token generation tasks, the external read vector $z_{\mathrm{corpus}}(x)$ must project coherently onto candidate vocabulary tokens. Letting $E \in \R^{d \times e}$ denote the vocabulary embedding matrix, we tie atom values via $v(\omega) = E[y_\omega] / \|E[y_\omega]\|$ and evaluate final logits as $Ez / \tau$. Under this parameter tying, isolating an atom $\omega^*$ aligns $z(x)$ directly with the corresponding target row of the vocabulary matrix.

\subsection{Signed Atom Spaces}
\label{sec:signed_spaces}

To support zero-retraining cancellation in external memory, we allow the reference measure $\nu$ to be \emph{signed}. Any finite signed measure decomposes as $\nu = \nu^+ - \nu^-$ with $\nu^+ \perp \nu^-$ (Hahn--Jordan); in the finite counting case used throughout this paper this is simply the split of per-atom masses into positive records and negative ``anti-atoms'', and no measure-theoretic machinery is needed. Defining the total variation $|\nu| = \nu^+ + \nu^-$, we assume $0 < Z_x^{\pm} = \int_\Om K_x\,d|\nu| < \infty$. The normalized signed kernel-weighted measure is:
\[
  d\mu_x^{\pm}(\omega) = \frac{K_x(\omega)}{Z_x^{\pm}}\big(d\nu^+(\omega)-d\nu^-(\omega)\big).
\]
Its total variation is normalized to unity, while its signed total mass varies in $[-1, 1]$; unlike $\mu_x$, it is not a probability measure.

\subsection{Null Atoms and Abstention}
\label{sec:null_atoms}

\rev{Because $\mu_x$ is a probability measure, a plain read always returns a convex combination of stored values, even when no stored atom is relevant to $x$. The measure view supplies the missing outcome directly. A \emph{null atom} $\omega_\varnothing$ is an atom with value $v(\omega_\varnothing)=\mathbf 0$, reference mass $\nu(\{\omega_\varnothing\})=m_\varnothing>0$, and a query-independent kernel $K_x(\omega_\varnothing)=e^{\theta/\tau}$; it behaves like a key whose similarity to every query is $\theta$. Adding it to $\Om$ gives
\[
  R(x;\Om\cup\{\omega_\varnothing\}) = M_x\,R(x;\Om), \qquad
  M_x \coloneqq \frac{Z_x}{Z_x+m_\varnothing e^{\theta/\tau}},
\]
so the read is shrunk toward the zero vector by the factor $M_x\in(0,1)$, the mass left on real atoms.}

\begin{proposition}[Null atom and the hard-threshold limit]
\label{prop:null_atom}
\rev{Let $\Om=\{\omega_1,\dots,\omega_N\}$ carry the counting measure, $K_x(\omega_i)=e^{s_i/\tau}$, and $m_\varnothing=1$. With $s_{(1)}=\max_i s_i$,
\[
  \sigma\!\left(\frac{s_{(1)}-\theta}{\tau}\right) \;\le\; M_x \;\le\; \sigma\!\left(\frac{s_{(1)}-\theta}{\tau}+\log N\right),
\]
where $\sigma$ is the logistic function. Consequently, for fixed $N$, $M_x\to 1$ as $\tau\to 0$ if $s_{(1)}>\theta$ and $M_x\to 0$ if $s_{(1)}<\theta$.}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\rev{$M_x=\sigma(\log Z_x-\theta/\tau)$ and $e^{s_{(1)}/\tau}\le Z_x\le N e^{s_{(1)}/\tau}$; $\sigma$ is increasing. The limits follow because the $\log N$ term is constant while $(s_{(1)}-\theta)/\tau\to\pm\infty$.} \qedhere
\end{proof}

\rev{The activation threshold of Section~\ref{sec:counterfact} (``read only if the top-1 cosine exceeds $\theta$'') is therefore not an external heuristic: it is the zero-temperature limit of a null atom at similarity $\theta$, and the null atom is its smooth relaxation. In the tri-space read of Section~\ref{sec:atomspaces}, placing one null atom in each space gives
\[
  z(x)=\sum_{s}\lambda_s(x)\,M_{x}^{s}\,R(x;\Om_s), \qquad A(x)\coloneqq\sum_s\lambda_s(x)\,M_x^s\in(0,1),
\]
and the system answers from memory only when $A(x)\ge\tfrac12$, falling back to the base model otherwise. Section~\ref{sec:trigating} evaluates this on out-of-scope queries.}

\section{Mathematical Conditions for Post-Hoc Knowledge Editing}
\label{sec:editing}

Let the query-key kernel be a scaled inner product:
\[
  K_x(\omega) = \exp\left( \frac{\langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega) \rangle}{\tau} \right),
\]
where $\phi_q : \mathcal X \to \R^m$ and $\phi_k : \Om \to \R^m$ are query and key encoders, and $\tau > 0$ is a temperature parameter. Base training optimizes $\phi_q$ and $\phi_k$ on an initial pool $\Om_{\mathrm{train}} \subset \Om$. Post-hoc editing requires that inserting a novel atom $\omega^* \notin \Om_{\mathrm{train}}$ at test time reliably captures probability mass $\mu_x(\omega^*)$ for associated queries $x^*$, without modifying encoder parameters.

\subsection{Why an Anchor-Based Lipschitz Argument Does Not Certify Insertion}
\label{sec:anchor_remark}

\rev{A natural first attempt bounds the score of a new atom $\omega^*$ through a nearby training anchor $\omega_0\in\Om_{\mathrm{train}}$: if $\phi_k$ is $L$-Lipschitz for a semantic pseudo-metric $d_\Om$, Cauchy--Schwarz gives $\langle\phi_q(x),\phi_k(\omega^*)\rangle\ge\langle\phi_q(x),\phi_k(\omega_0)\rangle-\|\phi_q(x)\|_2\,L\,d_\Om(\omega^*,\omega_0)$, and one would require the right-hand side to exceed $\tau_k(x)$, the $k$-th largest score over $\Om_{\mathrm{train}}$. For $k=1$ this can never hold: $\omega_0\in\Om_{\mathrm{train}}$ implies $\langle\phi_q(x),\phi_k(\omega_0)\rangle\le\tau_1(x)$, and the penalty is non-negative. For $k>1$ it requires the anchor itself to rank above the $k$-th competitor by more than the penalty. On the 200-record benchmark of Section~\ref{sec:synthetic_insertion} (frozen MiniLM metric, empirical $\hat L=2.24$, mean nearest-anchor distance $1.04$) the penalty exceeds every observed margin and the condition holds for 0\% of records at $k\in\{3,5,10\}$ while actual top-1 entry is 100\%. The anchor construction is the wrong object: it asks a \emph{different} atom to vouch for $\omega^*$. The certificate below instead fixes the record and bounds how far the query may move.}

\subsection{A Query-Side Certified Radius}

\begin{proposition}[Query-side certified radius]
\label{prop:query_radius}
\rev{Let the stored keys $k_1,\dots,k_N\in\R^m$ be unit vectors and let $q_0=\phi_q(x_0)$ be the embedding of record $i$'s canonical query, with scores $s_j=\langle q_0,k_j\rangle$ and $s_i>s_j$ for all $j\ne i$. Define
\[
  r_i \coloneqq \min_{j\ne i}\frac{s_i-s_j}{\|k_i-k_j\|_2}, \qquad r_i^{\theta}\coloneqq\min\{r_i,\; s_i-\theta\}\quad(\theta<s_i).
\]
For every query $x$ with $\delta\coloneqq\phi_q(x)-q_0$: if $\|\delta\|_2<r_i$, record $i$ is the unique top-1 key for $x$; if in addition $\|\delta\|_2\le r_i^{\theta}$, then $\langle\phi_q(x),k_i\rangle\ge\theta$, so a gate at threshold $\theta$ activates on the correct record.}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\rev{For $j\ne i$, $\langle\phi_q(x),k_i-k_j\rangle=(s_i-s_j)+\langle\delta,k_i-k_j\rangle\ge(s_i-s_j)-\|\delta\|_2\|k_i-k_j\|_2>0$ whenever $\|\delta\|_2<r_i$. Since $\|k_i\|_2=1$, $\langle\phi_q(x),k_i\rangle\ge s_i-\|\delta\|_2\ge\theta$ whenever $\|\delta\|_2\le s_i-\theta$.} \qedhere
\end{proof}

\rev{The radius is attained: displacing $q_0$ along $(k_{j^*}-k_i)/\|k_{j^*}-k_i\|_2$ for the minimizing $j^*$ makes $j^*$ overtake $i$ at distance exactly $r_i$. Unlike the anchor argument, the certificate uses no Lipschitz premise and no training atom; it needs only the stored keys and one canonical query embedding, all available when the record is inserted, at $\mathcal O(Nm)$ cost. Inserting a later record can only shrink existing radii, by one additional term in each minimum. What the certificate does not provide is a link to meaning: it bounds displacement in the query encoder's space, and whether a paraphrase falls inside the ball is an empirical question about the encoder. Section~\ref{sec:counterfact} measures this on CounterFact.}

\subsection{Anti-Atoms and Exact External-Read Cancellation}

The following observation is algebraically immediate; we record it because it is the exact identity that the stress test of Section~\ref{sec:unlearning} perturbs.

\begin{proposition}[Matched-Pair Cancellation]
\label{prop:unlearning}
Let an atom $\omega^* \in \Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}$ encode a target fact with tied token value $v^* = E[y^*] / \|E[y^*]\|$. If an anti-atom $\omega^-$ is inserted into the signed atom space with identical key $\phi_k(\omega^-) = \phi_k(\omega^*)$, identical value $v^- = v^*$, and equal positive masses $m^* \coloneqq \nu^+(\{\omega^*\}) = m^- \coloneqq \nu^-(\{\omega^-\}) > 0$, then for any query $x$:
\[
  R^{\pm}(x; \{\omega^*, \omega^-\}) = \mathbf{0}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under the signed measure integral of Section~\ref{sec:signed_spaces}:
\[
  \int_{\{\omega^*, \omega^-\}} v(\omega)\, d\mu_x^{\pm}(\omega) = \frac{K_x(\omega^*) m^* v^* - K_x(\omega^-) m^- v^*}{Z_x^{\pm}} = \frac{K_x(\omega^*) m^* (v^* - v^*)}{Z_x^{\pm}} = \mathbf{0}.
\]
This establishes exact cancellation of the matched pair's contribution to the signed external read. \qedhere
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:unlearning} does not imply that the complete model representation is zero: background atoms, context, parameter experts, residual model states, and output bias remain. The positive record and anti-atom remain stored, and the proposition does not claim removal of knowledge compiled into dense pretrained parameters.

\begin{proposition}[Approximate Pair Cancellation]
\label{prop:approximate_cancellation}
Let $a=K_x(\omega^*)\nu^+(\{\omega^*\})$, $b=K_x(\omega^-)\nu^-(\{\omega^-\})$, and let $Z_x^{\pm}>0$ be the total-variation normalization. For positive value $v$ and anti-atom value $\tilde v$, the residual pair contribution satisfies:
\[
  \left\|\frac{a v-b\tilde v}{Z_x^{\pm}}\right\|_2
  \le \frac{|a-b|\,\|v\|_2+b\,\|v-\tilde v\|_2}{Z_x^{\pm}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $a v-b\tilde v=(a-b)v+b(v-\tilde v)$ and apply the triangle inequality, then divide by $Z_x^{\pm}$. \qedhere
\end{proof}

Exact cancellation is sensitive to key-induced kernel mismatch, unequal mass, and value mismatch. Paraphrases affect cancellation only through their effect on kernel weights; identical keys cancel for all queries.

\rev{Propositions~\ref{prop:unlearning} and~\ref{prop:approximate_cancellation} concern the matched pair in isolation. The question a practitioner asks is different: why not simply delete the record? The full read answers it.}

\begin{proposition}[Anti-atom equals deletion plus a null mass]
\label{prop:anti_vs_delete}
\rev{Let $\Om=\Om_o\cup\{\omega^*\}$ with $\nu(\{\omega^*\})=m>0$, and let $\omega^-$ be a matched anti-atom (same key and value, mass $m$ under $\nu^-$). Write $a=K_x(\omega^*)\,m$ and $Z_o=\int_{\Om_o}K_x\,d\nu$. Then for every query $x$,
\[
  R^{\pm}(x;\Om\cup\{\omega^-\}) \;=\; \frac{Z_o}{Z_o+2a}\;R(x;\Om_o),
\]
where $R(x;\Om_o)$ is the read after deleting $\omega^*$. Equivalently, the anti-atom read equals the deletion read with a null atom of mass $2m$ placed at the key of $\omega^*$.}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
\rev{The numerator is $\int_{\Om_o}K_x v\,d\nu+a v^*-a v^*$ and the total-variation normalizer is $Z_o+a+a$. Dividing gives $\frac{Z_o}{Z_o+2a}\cdot\frac{1}{Z_o}\int_{\Om_o}K_x v\,d\nu$.} \qedhere
\end{proof}

\rev{The two operations therefore coincide for queries far from $\omega^*$ ($a\ll Z_o$) and differ exactly near it. There, deletion returns $R(x;\Om_o)$, a renormalized read of the \emph{remaining} records: confident if some remaining key is close to the query, diffuse otherwise, but in either case an answer drawn from records unrelated to the removed fact. The anti-atom returns the same vector scaled by $Z_o/(Z_o+2a)\approx0$, that is, it abstains (Section~\ref{sec:null_atoms}). The second operational difference is bookkeeping: an anti-atom suits append-only or shared stores and is reversible (removing $\omega^-$ restores $\omega^*$ exactly), whereas deletion requires a mutable store and is not reversible from the store alone. Neither operation touches knowledge compiled into the base model's weights. Section~\ref{sec:unlearning} compares the two empirically.}

\section{Softmax Tail Truncation: An Exact Certificate}
\label{sec:quadrature}

Evaluating $R(x; \Om)$ densely over a large corpus is expensive, and practical systems read only the top-$k$ ranked atoms. We ask what can be \emph{certified} about the resulting error once the scores are known. \rev{The bounds in this section follow from standard inequalities and we claim no novelty for them; we include them because the computable quantity $Z_{\mathrm{tail}}/Z$ they expose gives a concrete stopping rule whose certified depth we then measure.} We analyze truncation to the exact ranked top-$k$ elements:
\[
  \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) = \sum_{i=1}^k v(\omega_{(i)})\, \frac{K_x(\omega_{(i)})}{\sum_{j=1}^k K_x(\omega_{(j)})}.
\]
In this discrete setting, numerical truncation corresponds to numerical quadrature of the query-conditioned measure against the value map, omitting the low-probability tail $Z_{\mathrm{tail}} = \sum_{i=k+1}^N e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}$. Dense scoring over $N$ items costs $\mathcal{O}(Ne)$ before the top-$k$ read. When paired with an ANN index, aggregation costs:
\[
  \mathcal{O}(c \cdot e) + \mathcal{O}(k_{\mathrm{corpus}} \cdot e) + \mathcal{O}(k_{\mathrm{params}} \cdot e).
\]

Let $N = |\Om|$, and $V_{\max} \coloneqq \sup_{\omega \in \Om} \|v(\omega)\|_2 < \infty$. For query $x$, let $s_i \coloneqq \langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega_i) \rangle$, with ordering $s_{(1)} \ge \dots \ge s_{(N)}$. Define $Z \coloneqq \sum_{j=1}^N e^{s_{(j)}/\tau}$ and $Z_k \coloneqq \sum_{j=1}^k e^{s_{(j)}/\tau}$. Define the retrieval spectral gap at rank $k$ as $\Delta_k(x) \coloneqq s_{(1)} - s_{(k+1)} \ge 0$.

\begin{proposition}[Softmax Tail Truncation Error Bound]
\label{thm:quadrature_bound}
For any query $x$ and truncation depth $k \in \{1, \dots, N-1\}$, the Euclidean approximation error of top-$k$ truncation satisfies:
\[
  \| R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) \|_2 \;\le\; 2 V_{\max} \cdot (N - k) \cdot \exp\left( -\frac{\Delta_k(x)}{\tau} \right).
\]
An alternative bound is:
\[
  \| R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) \|_2 \;\le\; 2 V_{\max} \cdot \left(\frac{N - k}{k}\right) \cdot \exp\left( -\frac{s_{(k)} - s_{(k+1)}}{\tau} \right).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Decompose the difference between the full read and the top-$k$ truncation:
\begin{align*}
  R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) 
  &= \sum_{i=1}^k v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau} \left( \frac{1}{Z} - \frac{1}{Z_k} \right) + \sum_{i=k+1}^N v_{(i)} \frac{e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}}{Z} \\
  &= \frac{Z_k - Z}{Z Z_k} \sum_{i=1}^k v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau} + \frac{1}{Z} \sum_{i=k+1}^N v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}.
\end{align*}
Let $Z_{\mathrm{tail}} \coloneqq Z - Z_k = \sum_{i=k+1}^N e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}$. Notice $\frac{1}{Z_k} \sum_{i=1}^k v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau} = \hat{R}_k(x; \Om)$, and define $R_{\mathrm{tail}} \coloneqq \frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}} \sum_{i=k+1}^N v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}$. Substituting yields:
\[
  R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) = \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z} \big( R_{\mathrm{tail}} - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) \big).
\]
Taking norms and applying the triangle inequality:
\[
  \| R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) \|_2 
  \le \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z} \big( \|R_{\mathrm{tail}}\|_2 + \|\hat{R}_k(x; \Om)\|_2 \big) 
  \le 2 V_{\max} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z}.
\]
Since $Z \ge e^{s_{(1)}/\tau}$ and $Z_{\mathrm{tail}} \le (N - k) e^{s_{(k+1)}/\tau}$:
\[
  \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z} \;\le\; \frac{(N - k) e^{s_{(k+1)}/\tau}}{e^{s_{(1)}/\tau}} \;=\; (N - k) \exp\left( -\frac{s_{(1)} - s_{(k+1)}}{\tau} \right).
\]
Substituting proves the first bound. Alternatively, $Z \ge k e^{s_{(k)}/\tau}$ while $Z_{\mathrm{tail}} \le (N-k) e^{s_{(k+1)}/\tau}$, giving
\[
  \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z} \le \frac{N-k}{k}\exp\left(-\frac{s_{(k)}-s_{(k+1)}}{\tau}\right),
\]
which proves the second bound. The second bound is tighter whenever $s_{(1)} - s_{(k)} < \tau \log k$.
\end{proof}

Both bounds are elementary consequences of $\|R-\hat R_k\|_2 \le 2V_{\max}\,Z_{\mathrm{tail}}/Z$. \rev{Any guarantee that follows from them holds by construction, so we do not treat empirical coverage as evidence.} Their use is that they require no distributional assumption on scores and that $Z_{\mathrm{tail}}/Z$ is directly computable, which yields the following stopping rule.

\begin{corollary}[Error-Budgeted Exact Truncation]
\label{cor:adaptive_k}
Let $\rho_k(x) = Z_{\mathrm{tail}}/Z$ be the exact softmax mass outside the first $k$ ranked atoms. For any requested $\varepsilon > 0$, define
\[
  k_\varepsilon(x) = \min\{k \in \{1,\dots,N\} : 2 V_{\max} \rho_k(x) \le \varepsilon\}.
\]
Then $\|R(x;\Om) - \hat R_{k_\varepsilon(x)}(x;\Om)\|_2 \le \varepsilon$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the proof of Proposition~\ref{thm:quadrature_bound}, $\|R - \hat R_k\|_2 \le 2 V_{\max} \rho_k(x)$. Substituting the condition for $k_\varepsilon(x)$ yields the result. \qedhere
\end{proof}

Computing $\rho_k(x)$ requires all $N$ scores, so Corollary~\ref{cor:adaptive_k} is a post-hoc certificate for a read that has already been scored densely (or whose candidate set is supplied by an approximate index), not a sublinear retrieval algorithm. Section~\ref{sec:empirical} shows that, on synthetic corpora, the certified $k_\varepsilon$ is a large fraction of $N$; we regard this as a useful calibration of how loose exact tail certification is relative to the aggressive truncation used in practice.

\section{Empirical Evaluation}
\label{sec:empirical}

We report (i) a saturated synthetic sanity check that also feeds the anchor remark of Section~\ref{sec:anchor_remark}, (ii) empirical behavior of the exact truncation certificate, (iii) CounterFact-500 retrieval with ROC threshold calibration, \rev{explicit counting rules, a failure analysis, and the query-side certificate}, (iv) a rank-one parametric reference and (v) GRACE \rev{scored, together with the read and the unedited model, by one scoring function}, (vi) tri-space routing \rev{with null-atom abstention}, (vii) signed-read cancellation under perturbation \rev{and against deletion}, and (viii) GPT-2 first-token steering. Items (i), (vi), and (viii) are mechanism studies on templated or small data and are not claims of state-of-the-art editing performance.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Corpus and Domain Design}
We construct a benchmark of 200 structured technical facts distributed across four domains: \emph{Astronomy \& Astrophysics}, \emph{Systems \& Computing}, \emph{Chemistry \& Materials Science}, and \emph{Molecular Biology \& Genetics}. The first 40 items per domain form the adapter-training pool; the remaining 10 items per domain serve as post-hoc insertions. Their labels remain in the global output vocabulary, making this a transductive label-space evaluation.

\paragraph{Encoder Architecture and Inductive Bias}
We employ a frozen sentence transformer (\texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, $d_{\mathrm{in}} = 384$) as the geometric backbone, paired with a metric-preserving residual adapter:
\[
  \phi(x) = \mathrm{Normalize}\big( x + 0.1 \cdot \mathrm{MLP}(x) \big),
\]
where the final MLP layer is zero-initialized. Values are tied to token embeddings: $v(\omega) = E[y_\omega] / \|E[y_\omega]\|$, with predictions evaluated as $p(y \mid x) \propto \langle z(x), E[y] \rangle / \tau$.

\paragraph{Reproducibility Environment}
Artifacts were generated using Python 3.11.9, PyTorch 2.6.0+cu124, NumPy 2.4.6, sentence-transformers 5.7.0, and transformers 4.48.3 on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Ti Laptop GPU (4 GB VRAM) with an AMD Ryzen 7 5800U host. Pinned versions are listed in \texttt{requirements.txt}. The routing sweep reports seeds $\{40,41,42,43,44\}$; the GRACE and rank-one runs use seed 42.

\subsection{Synthetic Insertion Sanity Check}
\label{sec:synthetic_insertion}

Both the trained residual read and a frozen-MiniLM exact 1-nearest-neighbor baseline score 100.0\% on all three metrics at every density, and inserting all 40 records sequentially without teardown (pool growing from 161 to 200 atoms) leaves every earlier insertion correctly classified with zero training-argmax changes across five seeds; target attention mass stays at $99.68\pm0.01\%$ (Table~\ref{tab:editing_triad}). The benchmark is therefore saturated: it establishes that the read behaves as intended and supplies the embeddings for the remark of Section~\ref{sec:anchor_remark}, but it does not show that the residual adapter improves over raw embeddings, and we draw no such conclusion. \rev{Because a frozen exact 1-NN already solves it, we treat it purely as a correctness check of the implementation; the substantive retrieval evidence is CounterFact (Section~\ref{sec:counterfact}).} The one non-trivial trend is that as pool density grows from 1 to 40 the product of adapter spectral norms rises from 0.469 to 1.003 while target attention mass falls from 99.97\% to 99.75\%: the adapter departs further from identity as it is asked to separate more records, at a small cost in mass concentration.

\subsection{CounterFact External-Memory Retrieval and Calibrated Activation}
\label{sec:counterfact}

We evaluate frozen MiniLM retrieval geometry on the first 500 CounterFact records \cite{meng2022locating}. Memory keys concatenate the rewrite prompt with its requested target; evaluation covers 500 rewrite prompts, 1000 paraphrases (2 per record), and 1000 neighborhood prompts (2 per record). A record is \emph{activated} for a query when the cosine similarity of the top-1 key exceeds a threshold $\theta$ (distinct from the softmax temperature $\tau$).

\paragraph{Counting rules}
\rev{Each query is assigned to exactly one outcome. For a rewrite or paraphrase prompt of record $i$, with top-1 key $\hat\jmath$ and top-1 cosine $s_{(1)}$: \emph{activated-correct} if $\hat\jmath=i$ and $s_{(1)}\ge\theta$; \emph{retrieved-rejected} if $\hat\jmath=i$ and $s_{(1)}<\theta$; \emph{wrong-record-activated} if $\hat\jmath\ne i$ and $s_{(1)}\ge\theta$; \emph{retrieval miss} if $\hat\jmath\ne i$ and $s_{(1)}<\theta$. ``Activated recall'' in Table~\ref{tab:counterfact_retrieval} is the activated-correct rate, so it requires both a correct retrieval and a passed threshold; ``exact top-1 recall'' is activated-correct plus retrieved-rejected. For a neighborhood prompt of record $i$: \emph{silent} if $s_{(1)}<\theta$; \emph{wrong-edit retrieval} if $\hat\jmath=i$ and $s_{(1)}\ge\theta$ (the edit the neighborhood is meant to be protected from would be injected); \emph{other false activation} if $\hat\jmath\ne i$ and $s_{(1)}\ge\theta$. ``Neighborhood false activation'' is the sum of the last two. Table~\ref{tab:counterfact_buckets} gives the full decomposition.}

A fixed $\theta=0.60$ discards most valid paraphrases (38.2\% activated). We therefore calibrate $\theta$ on held-out data: records 500--999 form the calibration split and records 0--499 the evaluation split. On the calibration split, sweeping $\theta$ over positive queries (rewrites and paraphrases) versus negative distractors (neighborhood queries) yields ROC AUC $0.817$; the same sweep on the evaluation split gives AUC $0.829$. Maximizing Youden's $J$ on the calibration split selects $\theta^* = 0.55$.

\paragraph{Where the gate fails}
\rev{Raising activated paraphrase recall is now a question about one bucket: 37.8\% of paraphrases are ranked first among all 500 keys yet fall below $\theta^*$. These are not retrieval failures; the ranking is right and the absolute similarity is low. Their median top-1 cosine is 0.474, against 0.637 for activated-correct paraphrases, while their median rank margin $s_{(1)}-s_{(2)}$ is 0.109. In CounterFact, 92.6\% of paraphrase prompts begin with a model-generated distractor sentence followed by the paraphrased prompt (for example, an unrelated narrative sentence before ``Danielle Darrieux, a native''). The presence of a prefix does not by itself separate the outcomes (92.1\% of retrieved-rejected versus 94.0\% of activated-correct paraphrases carry one); its length and content do. Retrieved-rejected paraphrases have longer prefixes (median 8 versus 6 words) and lower token overlap with the stored key (median Jaccard 0.20 vs.\ 0.27), and every paraphrase in every bucket mentions its subject. Re-scoring only the final sentence of each paraphrase, with keys and $\theta^*$ unchanged, raises activated recall from 53.2\% to 91.6\% and recovers 89.9\% of the retrieved-rejected queries, while exact top-1 recall rises from 91.0\% to 95.0\%. The distractor sentence therefore lowers the absolute similarity much more than it disturbs the ranking. This is what the absolute threshold fails to recognize, and it suggests the relative statistic below. A gate on the rank margin instead of the absolute similarity, calibrated on the same held-out split, gives 70.8\% activated paraphrase recall at 7.0\% neighborhood false activation (absolute gate: 53.2\% at 16.1\%), better on both axes, with held-out AUC 0.914 against 0.829. The margin depends on the rest of the store, so its calibration would have to be revisited as the store grows denser; we report it as a diagnostic of what the absolute gate discards rather than as a proposed method. Under the absolute gate at $\theta^*$, the remaining paraphrases are genuine retrieval misses (8.4\%) or retrieve and activate a wrong record (0.6\%). Representative examples of each bucket are listed in \nolinkurl{results/details/counterfact_failure_outputs/examples.json}.}

\paragraph{Stored-key construction}
\rev{Keys concatenate the rewrite prompt with its \emph{new} target, which a paraphrase query never contains, so every positive similarity is depressed by the target tokens. With prompt-only keys, each recalibrated on records 500--999, mean paraphrase-to-own-key similarity changes from 0.552 to 0.607, the held-out threshold becomes 0.570, and activated paraphrase recall is 62.8\% at 19.0\% neighborhood false activation. Prompt-only keys are not a free improvement: two records that rewrite the same subject and relation would become indistinguishable, which is why the submitted construction keeps the target. The comparison separates the cost of the representation from the cost of the threshold decision.}

\paragraph{The additional false activations}
\rev{Lowering $\theta$ from 0.60 to 0.55 adds 6.5 points of neighborhood false activation (65 queries). Of these, 13.8\% retrieve the edited record itself and 87.7\% retrieve a record with the same CounterFact relation (against a 3.4\% chance rate for a random pair). CounterFact neighborhood prompts share the relation template of the edited record with a different subject, so a same-relation rate 26 times the chance rate means the gate is responding to the template rather than to the subject. This is the specific cost of the operating point $\theta^*$: the additional false activations lie in a narrow band just below the old threshold (median top-1 cosine 0.574), the same band as the recovered paraphrases, and differ from them in the subject, which a single cosine threshold over whole-sentence embeddings does not isolate. The rank-margin gate above reduces false activations to 7.0\%, consistent with template matches being close to several same-relation keys at once and therefore having small margins.}

\paragraph{Certified coverage}
\rev{Proposition~\ref{prop:query_radius} is checkable on every record. 99.2\% of records are top-1 for their own rewrite prompt, the median certified radius is 0.397 (activation radius at $\theta^*$: 0.324), and the median paraphrase displacement $\|\phi_q(x)-q_0\|_2$ is 0.879. The certificate therefore covers 0.7\% of paraphrases for top-1 and 0.3\% for activation (actual: 91.0\% and 53.2\%), with no violations; after removing the distractor prefix these become 38.7\% and 26.9\%. Unlike the anchor condition of Section~\ref{sec:anchor_remark}, the certificate is satisfiable and holds where it applies; the gap to actual coverage measures how far CounterFact paraphrases move in the encoder's space relative to the inter-key margins.}

\subsection{Rank-One Covariance-Regularized Parametric Reference}
\label{sec:counterfact_rome}

To place the retrieval numbers beside a parametric edit at the same scale, we implement a rank-one covariance-regularized (R1C) update on GPT-2 (124M) for the same 500 CounterFact records. The construction follows the ROME update rule \cite{meng2022locating}: the edit is applied at the subject span found via tokenizer offsets; a single global edit layer (layer 3) is chosen by a causal trace averaged over 50 held-out records; and the update is $W \leftarrow W + (C^{-1}k)(v^*-k^{\top}W)^{\top}/(k^{\top}C^{-1}k)$ with $C=\mathbb{E}[kk^{\top}]$ estimated on 1,500 held-out texts. It departs from the published ROME protocol in four ways: one canonical prompt per edit, no multi-template key averaging or KL regularizer, a CounterFact-derived covariance corpus, and first-subtoken rather than full-sequence target scores. We therefore do not call it ROME and do not compare it to published ROME numbers.

On CounterFact-500, R1C attains an 85.2\% efficacy score (35.6\% argmax), 59.5\% paraphrase score, and 70.1\% neighborhood score, with control-text perplexity ratio 1.001 mean and 1.291 max. Its paraphrase score is compared with GRACE in the next subsection. \rev{Its neighborhood score (70.1\%) is a different quantity from the read's neighborhood false activation (16.1\%); the comparable figure for the read, its own neighborhood score under the same scorer, is reported in Table~\ref{tab:grace}.}

\subsection{GRACE at Matched Scale}
\label{sec:grace}

GRACE \cite{hartvigsen2023aging} is the memory-based editor closest in mechanism to the gated read: it stores a codebook of (key, value, $\epsilon$) triples at one MLP layer, fires when the last-token input activation lies within $\epsilon$ of a stored key, and replaces that token's layer output by the stored value. Its $\epsilon$-ball plays the role of our activation threshold $\theta$, with keys in a hidden-activation space rather than a sentence-embedding space. We re-implement GRACE in our own code following the algorithm and default hyper-parameters of the reference implementation, rather than running the authors' released code, on GPT-2 (124M) at layer 9 of 12 (the relative depth of the layer used for GPT-2 XL), with $\epsilon_{\mathrm{init}}\in\{1,3,10\}$, SGD value training (learning rate 1.0, 100 iterations) on the first-subtoken cross-entropy, and GRACE's expand/split rule on key conflicts. Edits are applied to the same 500 records with the same scorer as R1C, under GRACE's native sequential protocol (all 500 edits accumulated, evaluation after the last) and under per-record reset. Because the scorer compares first-subtoken probabilities rather than the full multi-token log-probabilities of the published CounterFact protocol, the numbers in Table~\ref{tab:grace} are comparable to each other and to Table~\ref{tab:counterfact_retrieval}, but not directly to efficacy, paraphrase, or neighborhood figures reported for ROME or GRACE elsewhere.

GRACE edits the rewrite prompt almost perfectly (100\% efficacy with reset, 99.2\% after 500 sequential edits) and leaves control perplexity exactly unchanged; the ratio of 1.000 is measured, but it is also forced by the mechanism, since no control-text activation falls inside any stored $\epsilon$-ball and the wrapped layer is then the identity. It does not generalize at all: paraphrase activation is 0.0--0.2\% and its paraphrase score, 28.1\%, equals the unedited model's. Widening $\epsilon_{\mathrm{init}}$ to 3 or 10 does not help, because GRACE's split rule shrinks conflicting balls to half the inter-key distance; after 500 edits the mean $\epsilon$ is 0.85, 1.01, and 1.02 for $\epsilon_{\mathrm{init}}=1,3,10$, paraphrase activation stays below 6\%, and neighborhood false activation rises to 13--14\%. In hidden-activation space the CounterFact paraphrases are simply not within reach of the rewrite key. The read operator, by contrast, activates on 53.2\% of paraphrases at a 16.1\% false-activation cost, and R1C's weight update reaches 59.5\% paraphrase score at the cost of a 35.6\% argmax rate and a 1.29 worst-case perplexity ratio.

\rev{The base and read columns are computed by \texttt{src/counterfact\_unified\_editors.py}, which calls the GRACE script's per-record scoring function unchanged. With all columns now scored identically, the read's own efficacy, paraphrase, and neighborhood scores can be read directly against R1C and GRACE. The read has the highest paraphrase score of the four systems (62.6\%, interval 59.3--65.9\%; R1C 59.5\%, GRACE 28.1\%) and the highest paraphrase argmax (44.2\% against 8.9\% for R1C), and the lowest neighborhood score (68.3\% against 71.3\% unedited, 70.1\% for R1C, and 71.2\% for GRACE-sequential). Its 16.1\% false activation therefore costs 3.0 points of neighborhood score: a false activation lowers this score only when the injected read favours the queried record's new target over its true one, so the two rates need not coincide. Its paraphrase score is capped by activation: paraphrases on which the gate does not fire score at the unedited rate.} Three protocol differences remain and are stated rather than removed: R1C is evaluated with a per-record reset, whereas GRACE-sequential and the read hold all 500 edits at once; GRACE's value is a trained hidden-state replacement, the read's a fixed token embedding; and R1C and GRACE modify the forward pass, while the read is applied only when its gate fires.

The comparison is informative about mechanism rather than about a winner. GRACE and the read are both threshold-gated lookups, and they sit at opposite ends of a locality trade-off that is set by the key space: hidden activations at one token give near-zero false activation and near-zero generalization, sentence embeddings give moderate generalization at moderate false activation, and the ROC-calibrated $\theta^*$ lets that trade-off be chosen from held-out data rather than fixed by a single $\epsilon_{\mathrm{init}}$ that the split rule then overrides. The comparison also shows what the read gives up: GRACE's value is a learned hidden-state replacement, whereas the read's value is a fixed token embedding mixed into the final state (Section~\ref{sec:generative}). On GPT-2 small the mixing weight $\lambda_{\mathrm{read}}=0.6$ gives 98.8\% rewrite argmax; GPT-2 XL needs a larger, separately calibrated weight (Section~\ref{sec:generative}). Neither mechanism edits the paraphrases that its gate does not reach.

\subsection{Dynamic Tri-Space Routing and Path Ablation}
\label{sec:trigating}

We evaluate the tri-space architecture across three information paths: in-context reasoning ($\Om_{\mathrm{ctx}}$, 15 ephemeral premises supplied at query time), corpus world knowledge ($\Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}$, 50 stored facts), and parameter-resident expert outputs ($\Om_{\mathrm{params}}$, 15 tasks served by a learned expert bank). No routing labels are used; route accuracy below is an emergent property of task supervision. Training queries use family-specific wrappers; evaluation uses unseen strings under the shared wrapper ``Determine the output for:'' across all three families, so that surface form cannot identify the intended space.

\paragraph{Exact cancellation}

\paragraph{Approximate cancellation stress test}
We perturb anti-atom keys, values, masses, or queries over 50 trials at each of background sizes 10, 100, and 1000 (3,000 trials) and compare the residual pair contribution with the bound of Proposition~\ref{prop:approximate_cancellation}. The bound holds in all trials. At $N=100$, key perturbations of $\ell_2$ magnitude 0.01, 0.05, and 0.10 leave mean pair residuals of 4.5\%, 72.1\%, and 98.0\% of the uncancelled contribution; because keys are unit-norm and the temperature is sharp, a perturbation of 0.05 already moves the anti-atom's kernel weight far from the record's. Query perturbations leave zero residual because identical keys receive identical kernel weights for every query. In practice, therefore, an anti-atom must be constructed from the stored key itself rather than re-encoded from text.

\paragraph{Anti-atom versus deletion}
\rev{Starting from the same store, we either delete each sensitive record (mass set to zero) or append its matched anti-atom (Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_vs_delete}). Both remove the sensitive target: its probability falls to 0.70\% (mean) after deletion and to $2.04\%$ after the anti-atom. They differ in what the query receives instead. After deletion, the read falls through to the remaining records: its norm is 0.23 on average (against $\approx$1.00 before), its argmax is another stored record's target in all five cases, and its top probability averages 0.13, about six times the uniform $1/49$ (normalized entropy 0.81). In this store no remaining key is close to the sensitive queries, so the fall-through answer is weak rather than confident. After the anti-atom, the read norm is $2.6\times10^{-5}$ and the output is uniform (normalized entropy 1.00). As Proposition~\ref{prop:anti_vs_delete} predicts, the two reads point in the same direction (the argmax coincides in all five cases) and differ only by the factor $Z_o/(Z_o+2a)\approx10^{-4}$. Specificity on the 35 background facts is 100.0\% in both arms. When the store is mutable and a fall-through answer is acceptable, deletion is simpler. When the query should receive no stored answer, or the store is append-only or must be reversible, the anti-atom is the operation that achieves it.}

\paragraph{Competitive updating}
Appending a contradicting atom with recency weight $w_{\mathrm{new}} = 2.5$ overrides the old fact on only $44.0\pm8.9\%$ of records across five seeds (Table~\ref{tab:unlearning_results}, bottom). Where it fails, the query is lexically closer to the original record than to the new one, and the recency weight does not compensate. Append-only updating without cancellation of the superseded record is therefore unreliable in this setting.

\subsection{First-Token Steering of GPT-2 as a Retrieval Diagnostic}
\label{sec:generative}

We couple $\Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}$ into the final hidden state of pretrained GPT-2 (124M \cite{radford2019language}) with cosine activation at $\theta \ge 0.60$:
\[
  \tilde{h}_t =
  \begin{cases}
    (1-\lambda_{\mathrm{read}})h_t + \lambda_{\mathrm{read}}W_{\mathrm{proj}}z_t,
      & \text{relevant and unconsumed},\\
    h_t, & \text{otherwise}.
  \end{cases}
\]
Each memory value is the unit-normalized output embedding of the target's first token. The construction is therefore circular by design: conditional on retrieval, injecting the target embedding into the final hidden state is expected to raise the target's first-token probability. The experiment does not test whether the model has ``learned'' a fact; it tests (a) whether the retrieval gate fires on the right prompts and stays silent on controls, and (b) how large a hidden-state perturbation is needed to flip the argmax. The probability ratios in Table~\ref{tab:generative_results} should be read in that light and not as evidence of editing quality.

\section{Discussion and Related Work}
\label{sec:discussion}

The computational elements unified under our framework have extensive histories across distinct areas of machine learning. External corpus reads $R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{corpus}})$ correspond to nearest-neighbor language models and retrieval-augmented architectures, such as $k$NN-LM \cite{khandelwal2020generalization, he2021efficient}, RETRO \cite{borgeaud2022improving}, and memory network designs \cite{sukhbaatar2015end, lample2019large}. Parameter-expert reads $R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{params}})$ encapsulate modular computation, including sparsely gated Mixture-of-Experts \cite{shazeer2017outrageously, fedus2022switch, jiang2024mixtral} and dynamic adapter routing banks \cite{hu2021lora}. Finally, standard self-attention \cite{vaswani2017attention} constitutes an in-context read $R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{ctx}})$ over sequence coordinates, whose continuous formulation relates naturally to kernel attention smoothing and linear attention mechanisms \cite{tsai2019transformer, katharopoulos2020transformers}.

\subsection{Relation to Memory-Based and Non-Parametric Model Editing}
The atom-space read operator connects directly to non-parametric, semi-parametric, and memory-augmented model editing strategies.

Semi-Parametric Edit Retrieval and Correction (SERAC) \cite{mitchell2022memory} caches edits externally and employs an auxiliary classifier to route queries between a base language model and a separate counterfactual sequence-to-sequence model. The read operator shares the objective of keeping base weights frozen, but its retrieval is a convex combination $R(x; \Om)$ that combines linearly within a shared representation space, avoiding a second autoregressive model. The trade-off is direct: SERAC retains full sequence-level generation by delegating altered queries to the counterfactual network, while the read performs a single-pass vector addition at the representation layer and, as Section~\ref{sec:generative} shows, does not generate coherent multi-token continuations.

GRACE \cite{hartvigsen2023aging} inserts norm-constrained key-value codebook patches into intermediate transformer activations, with $\epsilon$-ball activation around stored keys. The activation gate $\theta$ of Section~\ref{sec:counterfact} plays the same role as GRACE's $\epsilon$; the differences are that our keys live in an external sentence-embedding space rather than a hidden layer, that cancellation is expressed on the reference measure rather than by codebook deletion, and that we report ROC-calibrated rather than fixed thresholds. Section~\ref{sec:grace} runs GRACE on the same 500 records with the same GPT-2 checkpoint and scorer and finds that the two gated lookups occupy opposite ends of a generalization--locality trade-off set by the key space. We have not run matched comparisons with SERAC, MEMIT \cite{meng2023mass}, MEND \cite{mitchell2022fast}, WISE \cite{wang2024wise}, or AlphaEdit \cite{fang2025alphaedit}, and nothing here should be read as a claim of superiority over them.

Interpolation between context attention and external memory stores has also been investigated in Memorizing Transformers \cite{wu2022memorizing} and adaptive $k$NN-LM \cite{he2021efficient}. The atom-space view places token context, dense external corpora, and parameter-expert banks under one read abstraction with separate normalization, which is the calibration principle of Section~\ref{sec:calibration}.

Finally, signed atom spaces interface with machine unlearning. SISA \cite{bourtoule2021machine} and Fisher-based certified removal adjust weights to eliminate the statistical influence of target data. Signed atom spaces cancel only in the read output, leave pretrained weights unmodified, and retain both the record and its anti-atom in storage; they are a mechanism for external-memory retraction, not for parametric unlearning.

\subsection{Relevance, Utility, and When to Retrieve}
\rev{The gate studied here decides \emph{relevance}: whether a stored key is close enough to the query to be read. Several lines of work address the neighbouring questions of whether retrieval is needed at all and whether retrieved or generated knowledge is useful once obtained. Mallen et al.\ \cite{mallen2023trust} show that the benefit of non-parametric memory depends on how well the parametric memory already covers a fact, and use this to decide adaptively when to retrieve; FLARE \cite{jiang2023active} triggers retrieval during generation when the model's own confidence drops; and Self-RAG \cite{asai2024selfrag} trains a model to emit reflection tokens that decide whether to retrieve and critique whether a passage supports the output. In the atom-space vocabulary these are learned, query-dependent versions of the gate $\lambda(x)$ and of the null atom: they decide how much mass to place on $\Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}$ versus $\Om_{\mathrm{params}}$ versus abstention, using signals (model confidence, utility judgements) that a similarity threshold does not see. On the utility side, GuideKG \cite{wei2024guidekg} scores generated knowledge sentences with a trained filter before fusing them into generation, and COSIGN \cite{li2024cosign} collects and organizes contextual facts from a knowledge graph before a generator uses them for completion. Both separate retrieving or producing candidate facts from deciding which ones help, the distinction our failure analysis makes concrete: the retrieved-rejected paraphrases of Section~\ref{sec:counterfact} are cases where the correct record is \emph{found} but the relevance gate declines it, while the extra false activations are cases where a relevant-looking record would not be \emph{useful}. Our framework analyses the read once these decisions are made; it does not learn them, and a learned utility signal of this kind is a natural replacement for the fixed similarity threshold.}

\subsection{Limitations}
The atom-space view is a shared notation; every result here is a finite-sum statement and none exploits a non-atomic measure. It does not imply that attention, RAG, and MoE are interchangeable. On the theoretical side, \rev{the propositions are elementary consequences of the definitions, and their value lies in what they let us state and measure rather than in their difficulty. The query-side radius certifies displacement in the encoder's space, not semantic equivalence, and on CounterFact it covers 0.7\% of paraphrases against 91\% actual top-1}; the truncation certificate requires all $N$ scores and selects 52--86\% of a synthetic corpus at $N=25{,}000$, and is reported as a calibration of how loose exact tail arguments are, not as an operational tool. \rev{Null-atom abstention uses one similarity threshold and is weak on near-miss queries that share a template with stored items; the out-of-scope sets are small.} The 200-fact benchmark and the tri-space routing tasks are templated; the insertion metrics are saturated, frozen 1-NN matches the trained read, and routing failures are benign only because the corpus and expert output vocabularies are disjoint. In the GPT-2 steering study the injected value is the target's own token embedding, so first-token gains are expected conditional on retrieval; the experiment measures gate behaviour rather than factual learning, and multi-token generation drifts or degenerates. The R1C reference is not a ROME reproduction, and the GRACE comparison is our own re-implementation on GPT-2 small at a proportionally chosen layer with a first-subtoken value objective rather than the authors' code in its published GPT-2 XL hallucination setup; all CounterFact scores in this paper use first-subtoken probability comparisons and are therefore internally consistent but not numerically interchangeable with published ROME or GRACE results. No comparison with SERAC, MEMIT, MEND, WISE, or AlphaEdit is provided, and the retrieval read is scored on first-subtoken metrics rather than as an end-to-end editor. \rev{Apart from the unified scoring on GPT-2 XL, experiments use GPT-2 (124M); R1C and GRACE were not rerun at that scale, and the read's injection weight does not transfer across model sizes and must be recalibrated per model ($\lambda_{\mathrm{read}}=0.6$ for GPT-2 small, $0.85$ for GPT-2 XL, chosen on held-out records).} \rev{The rank-margin gate is evaluated on one store size and is a diagnostic, not a tested method.} Finally, anti-atoms retain both records, cancel only the external read, require the exact stored key, and do not remove knowledge from dense parameters.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have used a single read operator over valued atom spaces as a common notation for attention, dense retrieval, and expert routing, \rev{and shown that a few operations on its reference measure express mechanisms usually treated separately. A null atom gives abstention, and the activation threshold of gated retrieval is its zero-temperature limit. A matched anti-atom is deletion plus a null mass, so it abstains exactly where deletion would fall through to an unrelated record. A query-side margin certifies, at insertion time and without a Lipschitz premise, a radius within which a record stays top-1 and activates; an anchor-based Lipschitz argument, by contrast, cannot certify top-1 entry at all.} The exact softmax tail certificate holds by construction but licenses far less truncation than practitioners use. On CounterFact-500, held-out ROC calibration of the activation threshold buys 15 points of paraphrase activation for 6.5 points of false activation. \rev{The remaining gap to 91\% exact top-1 recall consists of paraphrases that are ranked correctly but fall below the threshold, largely because CounterFact's distractor prefixes lower absolute similarity more than they disturb the ranking: scoring only the final sentence recovers 90\% of them, and a rank-margin gate reaches 70.8\% activation at 7.0\% false activation, better on both axes than the absolute gate. The extra false activations bought by calibration retrieve a same-relation record 88\% of the time, against 3\% by chance, so the gate responds to relation templates rather than to subjects.} Under one scorer on the same records, GRACE's hidden-activation codebook reaches 99--100\% rewrite efficacy with no measurable side effects but fires on almost no paraphrases, which places the two gated lookups at opposite ends of a trade-off governed by the key space rather than by the gating rule. The next steps are subject-sensitive, relative, or learned relevance gates, tail bounds under explicit score-distribution assumptions, and matched comparisons with MEMIT and SERAC under their published protocols.
