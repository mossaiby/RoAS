\subsection*{Motivation and Interdisciplinary Origin}

While my formal academic affiliation is within Civil Engineering, my core research specialization lies in \textbf{computational mechanics, meshfree particle methods, and high-performance scientific computing}. In numerical continuum mechanics, meshfree particle methods evaluate continuous field quantities by integrating smoothing kernel functions against scattered particle values over spatial domains:
\[
  A(\mathbf{x}) \approx \int_{\Omega} A(\mathbf{x}') W(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}', h)\, d\mathbf{x}' \approx \sum_{j} A_j W(\mathbf{x} - \mathbf{x}_j, h) V_j
\]
Upon investigating the mathematical mechanics of modern autoregressive Transformers and Large Language Models (LLMs), I recognized that the attention mechanism---typically formalized as discrete matrix multiplications over sequence coordinates---is conceptually similar to non-local kernel integration over scattered particle domains. In this mapping, queries act as field coordinates, keys act as particle positions, values represent discrete field quantities, and the softmax attention density corresponds to a partition-of-unity smoothing kernel.

Motivated by this continuum-mechanics perspective, I reformulated language model inference not as an ad-hoc sequence operation, but as a continuous integral of a query-conditioned probability measure against a vector value map over an arbitrary \textbf{``atom space''} $(\Omega, \mathcal{F}, v)$.

To bridge this mechanics-inspired formulation with modern deep learning and natural language processing standards, I collaborated interactively with an AI research assistant (Google Gemini). This dialogue assisted in formalizing the machine-learning terminology, deriving the analytical quadrature bounds, and engineering the PyTorch benchmark suites (in full compliance with Elsevier's policy on Generative AI).

\subsection*{Core Scientific Contributions}

This manuscript presents several theoretical and empirical contributions that will be of strong interest to the readership of \emph{Neurocomputing}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.6em, itemsep=0.3em]
  \item \textbf{Unification of Context, Retrieval, and Parameters:} We formulate in-context attention, corpus retrieval, and parameter-resident expert routing as instances of one \textbf{Read Operator} $R(x; \Omega)$, with at least 91.8\% routing allocation to the intended space in the reported experiment.
  \item \textbf{The Quadrature Truncation Bound Theorem:} We prove a closed-form exponential bound for exact ranked top-$k$ truncation under calibrated kernel temperatures ($\tau = 0.05$, measured error $\approx 10^{-6}$). ANN candidate retrieval is not benchmarked.
  \item \textbf{Lifelong Scaling Without Observed Interference:} Forty sequential additions expand active memory to 200 atoms while retaining 100\% recall and background specificity on the reported benchmark.
  \item \textbf{Signed External-Memory Suppression:} Matched negative ``anti-atoms'' cancel selected external-read contributions, driving target probability to uniform entropy ($1/|\mathcal{V}| = 2.04\%$) without retraining; this does not remove information from pretrained weights.
  \item \textbf{Autoregressive Generative Steering:} Relevance-gated atom injection into \textbf{GPT-2 (124M)} boosts first-token target probabilities by up to $1100\times$ and flips the first prediction on all five evaluated factual prompts.
\end{enumerate}
