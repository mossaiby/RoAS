\section*{Summary of the second revision}

Reviewer~1 recommended minor revision and raised three specific points. All three identified real problems, and each has been corrected.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{What triggers fallback after an anti-atom.} The previous text described the anti-atom's attenuated read as an abstention, although its argmax equals that of deletion. The revision makes the decision rule explicit. A fallback is triggered by a test on the read's net signed mass, the same rule the null-atom router already uses, and not by the attenuation itself. Proposition~5 now states this mass, which is $1$ after deletion and close to $0$ after an anti-atom. The experiment now records it: the test answers from memory on all five deleted records and falls back on all five anti-atom queries.
\item \textbf{Paraphrase score versus activation.} The statement that the read's paraphrase score is capped by its activation rate was incorrect, since unfired queries keep the unedited model's score. A new per-query analysis gives the exact split. Of the 62.6\% score, 49.7 points come from fired queries and 12.9 from unfired ones, which match the unedited model on every query. The bound is now stated for argmax accuracy, where it does hold.
\item \textbf{An inconsistency between Table~7 and Figure~1.} Table~7 carried values from an earlier run of the routing sweep. It now uses the committed result file from which Figure~1 is drawn. No conclusion changes.
\end{enumerate}

Reviewer~4 questioned the novelty of the framework, its operational relevance, and the size of its empirical gains. The response answers each point. In reply to the concern that the manuscript has no operational knowledge component, the revision adds a short section (Section~7.2, Table~12). It sets out the paper's results as operations on an external knowledge store: inserting, deciding relevance, abstaining, retracting, updating, and reading. For each operation it gives the rule that governs it and the evidence and measured failure mode in the paper. The section summarizes reported results and introduces no new claims.

\section*{Contribution}

The paper does not propose a new editing method, and the manuscript says so. Its contribution is analytical and empirical. Operations on the reference measure of a single read operator express abstention, retraction, and calibrated relevance for context, external memory, and parameter experts on one footing. From these follow exact results: the activation threshold as the limit of a null atom, anti-atom as deletion plus a null mass, and a satisfiable insertion certificate. On CounterFact, the paper locates the gap between retrieval and activation in the relevance statistic rather than in retrieval itself. A rank-margin gate reaches 70.8\% paraphrase activation at 7.0\% false activation, against 53.2\% at 16.1\% for the calibrated absolute threshold. The paper also compares the gated read, a rank-one parametric update, and GRACE under one scorer. I believe this kind of precise account of what reads over knowledge stores can guarantee, together with the measured operating points, is relevant to the readership of \emph{Knowledge-Based Systems}.

\section*{Ongoing work}

The framework is the basis of a separate system study now in progress (code and draft: \url{https://github.com/mossaiby/RoHAS}). There, the null atom of this manuscript is the abstention outcome of a per-token gate over heterogeneous experts, trained without routing labels. In preliminary experiments at small scale, a compiled arithmetic expert added to a frozen gate over pretrained models makes their arithmetic exact on held-out long equations at negligible cost elsewhere. New facts are added by updating one expert while compute-matched monolithic models forget. I mention this only as an indication that the operations analysed here support a working system. Those results are not part of the present submission and are being extended to larger scale.

All new results for the present manuscript are produced by scripts in the public repository, \url{https://github.com/mossaiby/RoAS}, which also contains the complete code, benchmark definitions, and result files.
