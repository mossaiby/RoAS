\title{Reads over Atom Spaces:\\
A Mathematical View of Context, Retrieval, and Parameter Routing in Language Models}

\author[inst1]{Farshid Mossaiby\corref{cor1}}
\ead{mossaiby@eng.ui.ac.ir}
\cortext[cor1]{Corresponding author.}
\affiliation[inst1]{organization={Department of Civil Engineering, University of Isfahan}, postcode={81744-73441}, city={Isfahan}, country={Iran}}

\begin{abstract}
Finite attention, external retrieval, and parameter-expert routing all use query-conditioned weighted reads, despite differing substantially in their internal computation and training objectives. We provide a common mathematical vocabulary for these mechanisms through valued atom spaces $(\Om,\mathcal F,\nu,v)$ and a Bochner read operator $R(x;\Om)=\int_\Om v(\omega)\,d\mu_x(\omega)$. In finite neural implementations, this read specializes to a softmax-weighted combination of value vectors. We derive two exact ranked top-$k$ truncation bounds and an error-budgeted stopping rule, a Lipschitz-margin condition sufficient for top-$k$ inclusion under an explicit global premise, and signed-read cancellation and perturbation bounds. The accompanying studies are mechanism diagnostics rather than a claim of a new general-purpose language-model architecture. A finite-dataset Lipschitz diagnostic is vacuous despite perfect synthetic top-1 entry, and exact tail certification requires all scores. On CounterFact-500, held-out ROC calibration ($\tau^*=0.55$, calibration AUC $=0.817$) raises activated paraphrase recall from 38.2\% to 53.2\%, while neighborhood false activation rises from 9.6\% to 16.1\%. Five-seed synthetic routing exposes corpus-to-parameter confusion, and matched signed pairs cancel only the external-read contribution, with cancellation degrading rapidly under key mismatch. A direct target-embedding intervention in GPT-2 improves first-token CounterFact scores on $N=50$ records but does not constitute an end-to-end comparison with retrieval-augmented generation or model editors. The framework clarifies shared structure and its operational limits, and is positioned relative to RAG and memory-based editing methods.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Autoregressive language models encode linguistic abstractions, syntax, and factual knowledge within dense parameter matrices \cite{vaswani2017attention, brown2020language, geva2021transformer}. While gradient-based optimization successfully acquires structural linguistic generalizations, compiling dynamic factual statements directly into static weights presents persistent operational challenges. Real-world facts obsolesce rapidly, require targeted localized updates, and are subject to regulatory erasure mandates (e.g., the ``Right to be Forgotten''). Consequently, post-hoc weight modification often triggers catastrophic interference, parameter drift, or high computational costs \cite{meng2022locating, meng2023mass}.

To address the rigidity of dense parameter storage, multiple architectural strategies have emerged across distinct sub-disciplines:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \emph{In-context reasoning} caches dynamic token representations in the key-value sequence memory \cite{vaswani2017attention}.
  \item \emph{Retrieval-augmented generation} (RAG) and nearest-neighbor language models ($k$NN-LM) decouple memory by querying external vector databases of textual passages \cite{lewis2020rag, khandelwal2020generalization, borgeaud2022improving}.
  \item \emph{Modular computation} mechanisms, including Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) and parameter-efficient adapter banks, dynamically route tokens across banks of specialized sub-networks \cite{shazeer2017outrageously, hu2021lora, jiang2024mixtral}.
\end{itemize}
While these techniques all rely on key-value similarity matching \cite{geva2021transformer, lample2019large, tsai2019transformer}, they are typically formulated using disconnected notations, disparate routing heuristics, and distinct operational semantics.

In this work, we present a unified mathematical formulation for in-context attention, external retrieval, and parameter-expert output routing. By expressing each mechanism through an integral of a query-conditioned probability measure against a value map over a valued atom space, we establish a shared analytical umbrella. In discrete neural architectures, this Bochner read operator specializes algebraically to query-conditioned softmax-weighted convex combinations over value representations.

Through this lens, we make the following contributions:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{A Unified Mathematical Formulation:} We define the read operator $R(x; \Om)$ over valued atom spaces $(\Om, \mathcal F, \nu, v)$ and formulate an integrated tri-space architecture that routes between context ($\Om_{\mathrm{ctx}}$), corpus retrieval ($\Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}$), and parameter experts ($\Om_{\mathrm{params}}$).
  \item \textbf{A Conditional Retrieval Guarantee and Finite-Dataset Diagnostic:} We derive a Lipschitz-margin condition sufficient for a novel atom to enter an exact top-$k$ set under an explicit global Lipschitz premise, then diagnose its conservatism with finite-dataset estimates.
  \item \textbf{Softmax Tail Truncation Bounds:} We prove Theorem~\ref{thm:quadrature_bound}, establishing two exponential tail bounds on exact ranked top-$k$ reads, and formulate Corollary~\ref{cor:adaptive_k} as an error-budgeted stopping rule.
  \item \textbf{Signed External-Memory Suppression:} By extending atom spaces to signed reference measures ($\nu = \nu^+ - \nu^-$), we formalize exact zero-retraining cancellation of external-memory contributions via anti-atoms, accompanied by an approximate cancellation perturbation bound.
  \item \textbf{Controlled Mechanism Studies:} Across five seeds, we evaluate templated cumulative insertion, multi-space routing, approximate cancellation stress tests, CounterFact retrieval with HNSW candidate indexing and ROC threshold calibration, and limited GPT-2 steering baselines. These studies identify practical failure modes and do not substitute for a matched end-to-end comparison with established editors.
\end{enumerate}

\section{From a Discrete Function to an Integral}
\label{sec:discrete_to_integral}

\subsection{The Token-Level Function}

A language model is conventionally defined as a conditional probability distribution over a finite vocabulary $\V$ of size $d$, given an input sequence. Fixing a maximum context capacity $c$ and introducing a null padding symbol $\varnothing$ yields an augmented vocabulary $\VBar = \V \cup \{\varnothing\}$. An input sequence is represented as a tuple
\[
  x = (x_1, \dots, x_c) \in \VBar^{\,c},
\]
where unassigned sequence positions are padded with $\varnothing$. A language model is a mapping
\[
  p : \VBar^{\,c} \to \Delta^{d-1}, \qquad
  \Delta^{d-1} = \Big\{ z \in \R^d : z_i \ge 0,\ \sum_{i=1}^d z_i = 1 \Big\}.
\]

\subsection{Attention as an Integral}

Let $\phi : \VBar \to \R^{e}$ denote an embedding map with $\phi(\varnothing) = 0$. Extending the sequence to a step function over the continuous interval $[0,c]$ gives:
\[
  f_x(t) = \phi\big(x_{\lceil t \rceil}\big), \qquad t \in (0,c],
\]
with $f_x(0) = \phi(x_1)$. Let $\alpha_x : [0,c] \to \R_{\ge 0}$ be an attention weight density satisfying $\int_0^c \alpha_x(t)\,dt = 1$, with $\alpha_x(t) = 0$ whenever $x_{\lceil t \rceil} = \varnothing$. The context representation vector is:
\[
  z(x) = \int_0^c \alpha_x(t)\, f_x(t)\, dt \in \R^e,
\]
and the corresponding token prediction distribution is $p(x) = \mathrm{softmax}\big( W z(x) + b \big)$.
Because $f_x$ is piecewise constant over unit intervals, the continuous integral evaluates algebraically to the standard discrete attention weighted sum \cite{vaswani2017attention, martins2020sparse}:
\[
  \int_0^c \alpha_x(t)\, f_x(t)\, dt = \sum_{i=1}^c \left( \int_{i-1}^i \alpha_x(t)\, dt \right) \phi(x_i) = \sum_{i=1}^c \alpha_i \phi(x_i).
\]
This continuous formulation naturally generalizes by replacing the sequence index interval $[0,c]$ with an arbitrary measurable \emph{atom space}.

\section{Atom Spaces: A Common Object for Context, Retrieval, and Parameters}
\label{sec:atomspaces}

\begin{definition}[Valued Atom Space]
A \emph{valued atom space} is a tuple $(\Om, \mathcal{F}, \nu, v)$, where $(\Om,\mathcal{F})$ is a measurable space of elements designated as \emph{atoms}, $\nu$ is a $\sigma$-finite reference measure, and $v : \Om \to \R^e$ is a measurable \emph{value map} into a common output representation space.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[Query-Conditioned Kernel and Read Operator]
Given a query $x\in\mathcal X$, let $K_x : \Om \to \R_{\ge 0}$ be measurable with
\[
  0 < Z_x \coloneqq \int_\Om K_x(\omega)\,d\nu(\omega) < \infty.
\]
Then $K_x$ induces a query-conditioned probability measure $\mu_x$ on $(\Om, \mathcal{F})$ via the Radon--Nikodym derivative:
\[
  d\mu_x(\omega) = \frac{K_x(\omega)}{Z_x}\, d\nu(\omega).
\]
If $\int_\Om\|v(\omega)\|_2\,d\mu_x(\omega)<\infty$, the \emph{read} conditioned on $x$ is the Bochner integral:
\[
  R(x; \Om) = \int_\Om v(\omega)\, d\mu_x(\omega) \in \R^e.
\]
\end{definition}

When $\nu$ is a discrete counting measure over a finite set $\Om = \{\omega_1, \dots, \omega_N\}$, the Bochner integral evaluates directly to a softmax-weighted sum:
\[
  R(x; \Om) = \sum_{i=1}^N \frac{K_x(\omega_i)}{\sum_{j=1}^N K_x(\omega_j)} v(\omega_i).
\]
As summarized in Table~\ref{tab:instances}, in-context attention, dense retrieval, and expert-output routing are structural realizations of this read operator, differing in their domain of integration and the query-dependence of their values.

\subsection{The Unified Architecture}

Context, external corpus memory, and parameter routing combine into an integrated representation:
\[
  z(x) = \lambda_{\mathrm{ctx}}(x) R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{ctx}}) 
       + \lambda_{\mathrm{corpus}}(x) R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}) 
       + \lambda_{\mathrm{params}}(x) R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{params}}),
\]
\[
  p(x) = \mathrm{softmax}\big( W_0\, z(x) + b_0 \big),
\]
where $\lambda(x) \in \Delta^2$ is an input-dependent gating distribution across the three spaces, and $W_0 \in \R^{d \times e}, b_0 \in \R^d$ represent the linguistic projection head. For parameter experts, the value is query-dependent, $v_x(\omega)=g_\omega(x)$; the read is therefore an output-space mixture, not an average of parameter tensors.

\subsection{System, Calibration, and Tying Principles}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Scale Mismatch and Norm Calibration:} The cardinalities of the spaces vary across orders of magnitude ($|\Om_{\mathrm{ctx}}| \sim 10^3$, $|\Om_{\mathrm{params}}| \sim 10^1$, $|\Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}| \sim 10^9$). Normalizing a single joint softmax across the disjoint union $\Om_{\mathrm{ctx}} \sqcup \Om_{\mathrm{corpus}} \sqcup \Om_{\mathrm{params}}$ causes the largest space to artificially dominate probability mass. Separate intra-space measure normalization combined with calibrated convex gating $\lambda(x)$ is required. Crucially, key representations across distinct spaces must share identical geometric scales (unit $L_2$-normalization) to prevent dot-product magnitudes from skewing the router toward unnormalized representations.
  \item \textbf{Tied Value-to-Token Embeddings:} In token generation, the read vector $z_{\mathrm{corpus}}(x)$ should project onto target vocabulary tokens. Letting $E \in \R^{d \times e}$ denote the vocabulary embedding matrix, we tie values via $v(\omega) = E[y_\omega] / \|E[y_\omega]\|$ and evaluate logits as $Ez / \tau$. Isolating atom $\omega^*$ aligns $z(x)$ directly with the target row.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Signed Atom Spaces and the Hahn--Jordan Decomposition}
\label{sec:signed_spaces}

To support zero-retraining cancellation in external memory, we extend the reference measure $\nu$ to a \emph{signed measure}. By the Hahn--Jordan Decomposition Theorem, any signed reference measure uniquely decomposes into mutually singular non-negative measures:
\[
  \nu = \nu^+ - \nu^-, \qquad \nu^+ \perp \nu^-,
\]
where $\nu^+$ governs positive constructive atoms and $\nu^-$ governs negative ``anti-atoms''. Defining the total variation measure $|\nu| = \nu^+ + \nu^-$, we assume $0 < Z_x^{\pm} = \int_\Om K_x\,d|\nu| < \infty$. The normalized signed kernel-weighted measure is:
\[
  d\mu_x^{\pm}(\omega) = \frac{K_x(\omega)}{Z_x^{\pm}}\big(d\nu^+(\omega)-d\nu^-(\omega)\big).
\]
Its total variation is normalized to unity, while its signed total mass varies in $[-1, 1]$; unlike $\mu_x$, it is not a probability measure.

\section{Mathematical Conditions for Post-Hoc Knowledge Editing}
\label{sec:editing}

Let the query-key kernel be a scaled inner product:
\[
  K_x(\omega) = \exp\left( \frac{\langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega) \rangle}{\tau} \right),
\]
where $\phi_q : \mathcal X \to \R^m$ and $\phi_k : \Om \to \R^m$ are query and key encoders, and $\tau > 0$ is a temperature parameter. Base training optimizes $\phi_q$ and $\phi_k$ on an initial pool $\Om_{\mathrm{train}} \subset \Om$. Post-hoc editing requires that inserting a novel atom $\omega^* \notin \Om_{\mathrm{train}}$ at test time reliably captures probability mass $\mu_x(\omega^*)$ for associated queries $x^*$, without modifying encoder parameters.

\subsection{Kernel Locality}

\begin{definition}[Metric Locality]
The key encoder $\phi_k$ is $L$-Lipschitz continuous with respect to a semantic pseudo-metric $d_\Om$ on $\Om$ if
\[
  \| \phi_k(\omega) - \phi_k(\omega') \|_2 \le L\, d_\Om(\omega, \omega') \qquad \forall\, \omega, \omega' \in \Om.
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{proposition}[Score Stability on Unseen Atoms]
\label{prop:score_control}
Let $\phi_k$ be $L$-Lipschitz with respect to $d_\Om$. For any training anchor $\omega_0 \in \Om_{\mathrm{train}}$ and any newly inserted atom $\omega^* \in \Om$:
\[
  \big| \langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega^*) \rangle - \langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega_0) \rangle \big| 
  \;\le\; \|\phi_q(x)\|_2 \, L \, d_\Om(\omega^*, \omega_0).
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
By the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality and the Lipschitz condition:
\begin{align*}
  \big| \langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega^*) - \phi_k(\omega_0) \rangle \big| 
  &\le \|\phi_q(x)\|_2 \, \|\phi_k(\omega^*) - \phi_k(\omega_0)\|_2 \\
  &\le \|\phi_q(x)\|_2 \, L \, d_\Om(\omega^*, \omega_0). \qedhere
\end{align*}
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Retrieval Margin Condition}

Let $\tau_k(x) = \max^{(k)}_{\omega \in \Om_{\mathrm{train}}} \langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega) \rangle$ denote the $k$-th highest score among existing training atoms. For the novel atom $\omega^*$ to enter the top-$k$ retrieved set under query $x$, it must satisfy $\langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega^*) \rangle > \tau_k(x)$. Combining this with Proposition~\ref{prop:score_control} yields a checkable sufficient condition:
\begin{equation}
  \langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega_0) \rangle - \|\phi_q(x)\|_2 \, L \, d_\Om(\omega^*, \omega_0) \;>\; \tau_k(x),
  \label{eq:margin_condition}
\end{equation}
where $\omega_0$ is a training anchor semantically proximal to $\omega^*$. Equation~\eqref{eq:margin_condition} is a sufficient condition for top-$k$ inclusion; successful prediction additionally depends on retrieved probability mass, value representation, output margin, and specificity on controls.

\paragraph{Finite-Dataset Diagnostic and Conservatism of the Margin Condition}
We diagnose Equation~\eqref{eq:margin_condition} on the finite benchmark. We instantiate $d_\Om(\omega, \omega') = \|\phi_0(\omega) - \phi_0(\omega')\|_2$ using the Euclidean distance between frozen MiniLM backbone embeddings. For the residual adapter $\phi(x) = \mathrm{Normalize}(x + 0.1 \cdot \mathrm{MLP}(x))$, an operator-norm calculation combined with the minimum adapted norm observed on this dataset gives a finite-dataset estimate $L \le 2.105$, while the maximum observed pairwise directional ratio is $\hat{L} = 2.236$. Neither quantity establishes the global premise of Proposition~\ref{prop:score_control}; they are diagnostics of its conservatism on the sampled records. Evaluating Equation~\eqref{eq:margin_condition} across all 40 insertion facts using their nearest training anchors $\omega_0$ reveals a stark contrast between observed entry and this diagnostic: actual top-1 entry is 100.0\%, whereas the margin is positive for 0.0\% of records across top-1, top-5, and top-10. Cauchy--Schwarz permits adversarial alignment in 384 dimensions; with inter-fact semantic distances averaging $d_\Om \approx 0.78$, the penalty $\hat L\,d_\Om \approx 1.74$ overwhelms the observed score margin ($\text{LHS} \approx -1.34$ versus $\tau_k \approx 0.35$). Thus, the experiment illustrates that a global worst-case condition can be vacuous on sparse semantic corpora; non-vacuous guarantees would require independently established local or directional bounds.

\subsection{Anti-Atoms and Exact External-Read Cancellation}

\begin{proposition}[Matched-Pair Cancellation]
\label{prop:unlearning}
Let an atom $\omega^* \in \Om_{\mathrm{corpus}}$ encode a target fact with tied token value $v^* = E[y^*] / \|E[y^*]\|$. If an anti-atom $\omega^-$ is inserted into the signed atom space with identical key $\phi_k(\omega^-) = \phi_k(\omega^*)$, identical value $v^- = v^*$, and equal positive masses $m^* \coloneqq \nu^+(\{\omega^*\}) = m^- \coloneqq \nu^-(\{\omega^-\}) > 0$, then for any query $x$:
\[
  R^{\pm}(x; \{\omega^*, \omega^-\}) = \mathbf{0}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under the signed measure integral of Section~\ref{sec:signed_spaces}:
\[
  \int_{\{\omega^*, \omega^-\}} v(\omega)\, d\mu_x^{\pm}(\omega) = \frac{K_x(\omega^*) m^* v^* - K_x(\omega^-) m^- v^*}{Z_x^{\pm}} = \frac{K_x(\omega^*) m^* (v^* - v^*)}{Z_x^{\pm}} = \mathbf{0}.
\]
This establishes exact cancellation of the matched pair's contribution to the signed external read. \qedhere
\end{proof}

Proposition~\ref{prop:unlearning} does not imply that the complete model representation is zero: background atoms, context, parameter experts, residual model states, and output bias remain. The positive record and anti-atom remain stored, and the proposition does not claim removal of knowledge compiled into dense pretrained parameters.

\begin{proposition}[Approximate Pair Cancellation]
\label{prop:approximate_cancellation}
Let $a=K_x(\omega^*)\nu^+(\{\omega^*\})$, $b=K_x(\omega^-)\nu^-(\{\omega^-\})$, and let $Z_x^{\pm}>0$ be the total-variation normalization. For positive value $v$ and anti-atom value $\tilde v$, the residual pair contribution satisfies:
\[
  \left\|\frac{a v-b\tilde v}{Z_x^{\pm}}\right\|_2
  \le \frac{|a-b|\,\|v\|_2+b\,\|v-\tilde v\|_2}{Z_x^{\pm}}.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Write $a v-b\tilde v=(a-b)v+b(v-\tilde v)$ and apply the triangle inequality, then divide by $Z_x^{\pm}$. \qedhere
\end{proof}

Exact cancellation is sensitive to key-induced kernel mismatch, unequal mass, and value mismatch. Paraphrases affect cancellation only through their effect on kernel weights; identical keys cancel for all queries.

\section{Computational Tractability and the Softmax Tail Truncation Bound}
\label{sec:quadrature}

Evaluating $R(x; \Om) = \int_\Om v(\omega)\, d\mu_x(\omega)$ densely over a large corpus is computationally expensive. We analyze numerical truncation to the exact ranked top-$k$ elements:
\[
  \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) = \sum_{i=1}^k v(\omega_{(i)})\, \frac{K_x(\omega_{(i)})}{\sum_{j=1}^k K_x(\omega_{(j)})}.
\]
In this discrete setting, numerical truncation corresponds to numerical quadrature of the query-conditioned measure against the value map, omitting the low-probability tail $Z_{\mathrm{tail}} = \sum_{i=k+1}^N e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}$. Dense scoring over $N$ items costs $\mathcal{O}(Ne)$ before the top-$k$ read. When paired with an ANN index, aggregation costs:
\[
  \mathcal{O}(c \cdot e) + \mathcal{O}(k_{\mathrm{corpus}} \cdot e) + \mathcal{O}(k_{\mathrm{params}} \cdot e).
\]

Let $N = |\Om|$, and $V_{\max} \coloneqq \sup_{\omega \in \Om} \|v(\omega)\|_2 < \infty$. For query $x$, let $s_i \coloneqq \langle \phi_q(x), \phi_k(\omega_i) \rangle$, with ordering $s_{(1)} \ge \dots \ge s_{(N)}$. Define $Z \coloneqq \sum_{j=1}^N e^{s_{(j)}/\tau}$ and $Z_k \coloneqq \sum_{j=1}^k e^{s_{(j)}/\tau}$. Define the retrieval spectral gap at rank $k$ as $\Delta_k(x) \coloneqq s_{(1)} - s_{(k+1)} \ge 0$.

\begin{theorem}[Softmax Tail Truncation Error Bound]
\label{thm:quadrature_bound}
For any query $x$ and truncation depth $k \in \{1, \dots, N-1\}$, the Euclidean approximation error of top-$k$ truncation satisfies:
\[
  \| R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) \|_2 \;\le\; 2 V_{\max} \cdot (N - k) \cdot \exp\left( -\frac{\Delta_k(x)}{\tau} \right).
\]
An alternative bound is:
\[
  \| R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) \|_2 \;\le\; 2 V_{\max} \cdot \left(\frac{N - k}{k}\right) \cdot \exp\left( -\frac{s_{(k)} - s_{(k+1)}}{\tau} \right).
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Decompose the difference between the full read and the top-$k$ truncation:
\begin{align*}
  R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) 
  &= \sum_{i=1}^k v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau} \left( \frac{1}{Z} - \frac{1}{Z_k} \right) + \sum_{i=k+1}^N v_{(i)} \frac{e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}}{Z} \\
  &= \frac{Z_k - Z}{Z Z_k} \sum_{i=1}^k v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau} + \frac{1}{Z} \sum_{i=k+1}^N v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}.
\end{align*}
Let $Z_{\mathrm{tail}} \coloneqq Z - Z_k = \sum_{i=k+1}^N e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}$. Notice $\frac{1}{Z_k} \sum_{i=1}^k v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau} = \hat{R}_k(x; \Om)$, and define $R_{\mathrm{tail}} \coloneqq \frac{1}{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}} \sum_{i=k+1}^N v_{(i)} e^{s_{(i)}/\tau}$. Substituting yields:
\[
  R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) = \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z} \big( R_{\mathrm{tail}} - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) \big).
\]
Taking norms and applying the triangle inequality:
\[
  \| R(x; \Om) - \hat{R}_k(x; \Om) \|_2 
  \le \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z} \big( \|R_{\mathrm{tail}}\|_2 + \|\hat{R}_k(x; \Om)\|_2 \big) 
  \le 2 V_{\max} \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z}.
\]
Since $Z \ge e^{s_{(1)}/\tau}$ and $Z_{\mathrm{tail}} \le (N - k) e^{s_{(k+1)}/\tau}$:
\[
  \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z} \;\le\; \frac{(N - k) e^{s_{(k+1)}/\tau}}{e^{s_{(1)}/\tau}} \;=\; (N - k) \exp\left( -\frac{s_{(1)} - s_{(k+1)}}{\tau} \right).
\]
Substituting proves the first bound. Alternatively, $Z \ge k e^{s_{(k)}/\tau}$ while $Z_{\mathrm{tail}} \le (N-k) e^{s_{(k+1)}/\tau}$, giving
\[
  \frac{Z_{\mathrm{tail}}}{Z} \le \frac{N-k}{k}\exp\left(-\frac{s_{(k)}-s_{(k+1)}}{\tau}\right),
\]
which proves the second bound. The second bound is tighter whenever $s_{(1)} - s_{(k)} < \tau \log k$.
\end{proof}

\begin{corollary}[Error-Budgeted Exact Truncation]
\label{cor:adaptive_k}
Let $\rho_k(x) = Z_{\mathrm{tail}}/Z$ be the exact softmax mass outside the first $k$ ranked atoms. For any requested $\varepsilon > 0$, define
\[
  k_\varepsilon(x) = \min\{k \in \{1,\dots,N\} : 2 V_{\max} \rho_k(x) \le \varepsilon\}.
\]
Then $\|R(x;\Om) - \hat R_{k_\varepsilon(x)}(x;\Om)\|_2 \le \varepsilon$.
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
By the proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:quadrature_bound}, $\|R - \hat R_k\|_2 \le 2 V_{\max} \rho_k(x)$. Substituting the condition for $k_\varepsilon(x)$ yields the result. \qedhere
\end{proof}

Theorem~\ref{thm:quadrature_bound} bounds truncation error once score ordering is known. Computing the certificate requires all scores, making it a certification rule rather than a sublinear algorithm.

\section{Empirical Evaluation}
\label{sec:empirical}

We examine inserted-record retrieval, adaptive exact top-$k$ truncation, CounterFact retrieval with HNSW candidates and ROC threshold calibration, cumulative insertion, tri-space routing, signed-read suppression under perturbation, and GPT-2 steering.

\subsection{Experimental Setup}

\paragraph{Corpus and Domain Design}
We construct a benchmark of 200 structured technical facts distributed across four domains: \emph{Astronomy \& Astrophysics}, \emph{Systems \& Computing}, \emph{Chemistry \& Materials Science}, and \emph{Molecular Biology \& Genetics}. The first 40 items per domain form the adapter-training pool; the remaining 10 items per domain serve as post-hoc insertions. Their labels remain in the global output vocabulary, making this a transductive label-space evaluation.

\paragraph{Encoder Architecture and Inductive Bias}
We employ a frozen sentence transformer (\texttt{sentence-transformers/all-MiniLM-L6-v2}, $d_{\mathrm{in}} = 384$) as the geometric backbone, paired with a metric-preserving residual adapter:
\[
  \phi(x) = \mathrm{Normalize}\big( x + 0.1 \cdot \mathrm{MLP}(x) \big),
\]
where the final MLP layer is zero-initialized. Values are tied to token embeddings: $v(\omega) = E[y_\omega] / \|E[y_\omega]\|$, with predictions evaluated as $p(y \mid x) \propto \langle z(x), E[y] \rangle / \tau$.

\paragraph{Reproducibility Environment}
Artifacts were generated using Python 3.11.9, PyTorch 2.6.0+cu124, NumPy 2.4.6, sentence-transformers 5.7.0, and transformers 4.48.3 on an NVIDIA GeForce RTX 3050 Ti Laptop GPU (4 GB VRAM) with an AMD Ryzen 7 5800U host. Pinned versions are listed in \texttt{requirements.txt}. The routing sweep reports seeds $\{40,41,42,43,44\}$.

\section{Discussion and Related Work}
\label{sec:discussion}

The components unified under our framework have extensive histories:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{Retrieval and Memory Networks:} $k$NN-LM \cite{khandelwal2020generalization, he2021efficient}, RETRO \cite{borgeaud2022improving}, and Memory Networks \cite{sukhbaatar2015end, lample2019large} correspond to corpus reads $R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{corpus}})$.
  \item \textbf{Parameter Routing and Mixture-of-Experts:} MoE architectures \cite{shazeer2017outrageously, fedus2022switch, jiang2024mixtral} and dynamic LoRA routing \cite{hu2021lora} represent realizations of $R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{params}})$.
  \item \textbf{In-Context Attention and Kernel Smoothing:} Standard self-attention \cite{vaswani2017attention} is an integral $R(x; \Om_{\mathrm{ctx}})$ over token coordinates, whose continuous interpretation relates to kernel attention formulations \cite{tsai2019transformer, katharopoulos2020transformers}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Relation to Memory-Based and Non-Parametric Model Editing}
The atom-space read operator directly connects to non-parametric and memory-augmented approaches:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.4em]
  \item \textbf{SERAC and Retrieval-Gated Editing:} Semi-Parametric Edit Retrieval and Correction (SERAC) \cite{mitchell2022memory} caches edits externally and uses a classifier to route queries between a base model and a separate counterfactual seq2seq model. RoAS shares the principle of preserving base weights, but formulates retrieval as a query-conditioned convex combination ($R(x; \Om)$) that injects linearly into a shared representation space, avoiding the overhead of maintaining two autoregressive models.
  \item \textbf{GRACE and Discrete Key-Value Adaptations:} GRACE \cite{hartvigsen2023aging} introduces local, norm-constrained key-value codebook patches into internal transformer activations for lifelong editing. While GRACE operates inside intermediate representations, RoAS externalizes factual records into measurable atom spaces with explicit Hahn--Jordan cancellation (anti-atoms) and error-budgeted top-$k$ truncation bounds.
  \item \textbf{Memorizing Transformers and Interpolated Retrieval:} Memorizing Transformers \cite{wu2022memorizing} and adaptive $k$NN-LM \cite{he2021efficient} interpolate between sequence attention and external retrieval. RoAS provides a unifying measure-theoretic framework that places token attention, dense external corpora, and parameter-expert banks under a common read abstraction with separate norm calibration.
  \item \textbf{Machine Unlearning:} Standard unlearning approaches, such as SISA \cite{bourtoule2021machine} or Fisher-based certified removal, modify network parameters to eliminate influence. Signed atom spaces ($\nu = \nu^+ - \nu^-$) instead accomplish output-space cancellation within the read operator, leaving pretrained parameter weights unmodified.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Limitations}
The atom-space formulation is a shared analytical lens, not a claim that attention, RAG, and MoE systems are algorithmically interchangeable. Synthetic studies use five seeds but remain templated; frozen 1-NN matches the trained read on synthetic insertions. The tri-space and GPT-2 experiments are controlled mechanism studies, and the latter directly mixes retrieved target embeddings into the final hidden state. CounterFact retrieval uses 500 records, while generative steering uses 50 records, and neither is compared end-to-end under matched protocols with SERAC, GRACE, MEMIT \cite{meng2023mass}, MEND \cite{mitchell2022fast}, WISE \cite{wang2024wise}, or AlphaEdit \cite{fang2025alphaedit}. The ROME-inspired baseline is not a reproduction of standard ROME. Exact adaptive certification requires computing all pairwise scores; its large selected fractions show it is an error-bound certificate rather than a sublinear algorithm. The finite-dataset Lipschitz diagnostic does not establish a global certificate. Anti-atoms retain both records, cancel only the external read under exact alignment, and do not remove information from dense parameters.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We have presented a mathematical formulation casting finite attention, dense retrieval, and expert routing as weighted reads over atom spaces. We derived a conditional retrieval margin result, exact-ranked truncation bounds, an adaptive error-budget rule, and an approximate signed-read cancellation bound, then used controlled studies to diagnose their behavior.

Error-budgeted exact truncation provides full empirical coverage while often selecting a large fraction of the corpus. CounterFact reveals strong exact retrieval, where data-calibrated thresholding improves paraphrase activation. The remaining studies demonstrate the limitations of templated routing, fragile cancellation under key mismatch, and first-token factual steering in GPT-2 via direct target-embedding injection. Establishing independently certified local retrieval conditions, sublinear guarantees, and matched end-to-end evaluations against competitive RAG and editing baselines are essential next steps.
